# Screening Room

Every few decades, someone tries to film *Dune*. Frank Herbert's 1965 novel has a way of swallowing the people who adapt it — its politics are too tangled, its hero too ambiguous, its best scenes take place inside people's heads. Alejandro Jodorowsky's version never made it to a camera. David Lynch's 1984 film made it to cinemas and left its director unhappy. Denis Villeneuve, splitting the book in two, finally gave it the room it seemed to need — and a third film, adapting *Dune Messiah*, is on its way.

This shelf collects reviews and essays about those attempts. They are written for pleasure, by a fan who has read the books too many times, and they are opinionated on purpose. Read them in any order; the list below goes from the films themselves to the ideas behind them, and ends with what comes next. One essay is written in Spanish, because Paul's story has a particular echo in Latin America.

Spoiler policy: the reviews discuss the films freely, but the biggest reveals are folded away where you can choose to open them.
