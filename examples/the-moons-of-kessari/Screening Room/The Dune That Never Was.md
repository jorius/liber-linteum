# The Dune That Never Was

In the mid-1970s, the Chilean-born filmmaker Alejandro Jodorowsky set out to adapt *Dune*, with the French producer Michel Seydoux behind him. He did not want to make a faithful adaptation. He wanted, by his own account, to make something closer to a prophecy — a film that would change the consciousness of the people who watched it. The film was never made. What survives is a legend, a huge book of storyboards, and Frank Pavich's documentary *Jodorowsky's Dune* (2013), in which the director, then in his eighties, tells the story with the energy of a man half his age.

## The team

The most remarkable thing about the project is the people Jodorowsky gathered, whom he calls his "spiritual warriors" in the documentary:

- **Moebius** (Jean Giraud), the French comics artist, who drew the storyboards.
- **H. R. Giger**, who designed for the Harkonnen world.
- **Chris Foss**, whose painted spaceships gave the project its colour.
- **Dan O'Bannon**, recruited for the special effects.
- For music, **Pink Floyd** and the French band **Magma**.
- In front of the camera, a planned cast that included **Salvador Dalí** as the Emperor, **Orson Welles** as the Baron and **Mick Jagger** as Feyd-Rautha, with Jodorowsky's own young son Brontis as Paul.

The money ran out before filming could begin; the studios Jodorowsky courted would not fund it. Several of the people he had brought together, though, went on to work together on Ridley Scott's *Alien* — O'Bannon as its writer, Giger as the designer of its creature — which is part of why the documentary can argue, with some justice, that the unmade *Dune* left fingerprints all over the science fiction that came after it.

> [!note] Not Herbert's book
> Jodorowsky took enormous liberties. The ending he describes in the documentary has Paul die and his consciousness pass into everyone around him, while Arrakis itself is transformed. It is beautiful as an idea and has almost nothing to do with Herbert. That is both the project's glory and the reason it is easier to love as a legend than it might have been as a film.

## The documentary

Pavich's film is less an investigation than a portrait of enthusiasm. Jodorowsky is a wonderful storyteller, and the documentary mostly lets him talk; the storyboards are animated just enough to suggest what might have been. It is ==one of the most joyful films ever made about failure==. Whether the unmade film would have been a masterpiece or a magnificent mess is impossible to say — and the documentary is wise enough not to pretend otherwise.

Watch it alongside Lynch's film and Villeneuve's and you get three very different ideas of what *Dune* is for: a visionary trip, a fever dream, a monument. Only one of them was never shot, and in a way it is the one that has been dreamed about the most. #jodorowsky
