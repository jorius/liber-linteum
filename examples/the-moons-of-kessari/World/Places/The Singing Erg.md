# The Singing Erg

South of the Salt Road, between the [[Cairn of Teeth]] and Dry Fork, the dunes grow huge: ridges two hundred feet high, their slip faces as steep and clean as poured honey. This is the **Singing Erg**, and at every Closing it sings.

The sound begins a little after the moons rise together: a low, steady hum, too low to be called a note, rising and falling like breathing. It can be heard a day's walk away, and in [[Glassreach]] the spires take it up and ring with it. Drovers caught in the erg at a Closing say the sand under their feet trembles, and that the hum seems to come up through the soles of their sandals rather than in through their ears.

## Why it sings

> [!note] Two explanations
> **The drovers:** the dunes are calling the water up from the deep, and the water answers.
>
> **The wellwrights:** the rising deep water shifts the sand from below, the steep faces start to slide, and sand that is dry and round enough *sings* as it slides, the way a wet finger sings on the rim of a glass.

Both, probably. See [[Kessari]] for the moons, and Ama's notes in [[The Dunes at Dusk]].

## Do not

- Do not cross the erg at a Closing.
- Do not camp at the foot of a slip face.
- Do not sing back. (A drovers' superstition. Nobody remembers why.)
