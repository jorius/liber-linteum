# Teodor Varn

| | |
|---|---|

| **Known for** | Speaking to a whole hall as if he had taken each person in it aside |
| **Now** | Gone south on a wind-skiff; whereabouts unknown |

**Teodor Varn** is the only son of [[Orla Varn]] and the heir of [[House Varn]]: tall, dark-coated, about thirty, the kind of man who smells faintly of water because he washes his hands in it and thinks nothing of it. He speaks for his mother at the Courts, and speaks well. Before 413 he was the best-liked member of the most disliked house at the Courts.

## What he believes

Teodor is not a fool and not, quite, a monster. He has read the old rolls as closely as any Keeper, and the lesson he has drawn from them is the Long Thirst of YW 113, when a city that found out there was more water than it had been told stopped paying, then stopped obeying, and two thousand people died in the Lees, almost none of them of thirst.

> [!quote] In [[04 Glassreach]]
> I sell them order. A number that everybody believes is worth more than a well.

> [!quote]
> Do you know what happens to a city when it learns there's more water than it was told? First it stops paying. Then it stops obeying.

## In the story

- Proposes the Glassreach cut at the sitting in [[02 Low Moon]], and asks the Loom House for one particular ledger
- Follows Ama to [[The Glass Annex]] by wind-skiff, offers her a post keeping Varn's books, and says six words she will not forget, in [[04 Glassreach]]
- Waits at the head of the well-stair at dawn in [[05 The Closing]], and steps aside
- Argues his case in [[06 What the Glass Remembers]], and loses it

> [!info]- Spoilers: what he knew
> Teodor did not begin the hidden draw; his mother did, in the Dry Year, when he was nineteen. He expanded it, built the trade in *sweetwater* in the Glass Market, and believed every word he said about order. Whether he knew what happened to [[Ilen Seyru]] at the [[Cairn of Teeth]] is not known. He knew the words the Varn rider used that night: *the road takes whom it takes*. He said them to Ama at the foot of the Annex stair. A week after the sitting of 413 he went south on a wind-skiff, and nobody has seen him since.
