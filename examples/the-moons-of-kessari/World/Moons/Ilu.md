# Ilu

**Ilu** is the larger and more distant of [[Kessari]]'s two moons: a pale, cream-white disc mottled with grey, so large when it rises that strangers to the desert stop and stare. It goes once round the world in $T_{\text{Ilu}} = 27$ days, and thirteen of its turns make the 351-day year.

## In the sky

Ilu is slow. It rises about fifty minutes later each night, and spends most of a night crossing the sky. When it is full and high, the dunes are bright enough to read a ledger by, and caravans prefer to travel on Ilu nights.

## In the calendar

The Keepers' year is thirteen *turns*, each named for what used to happen in it before the Lean Years: the Turn of Salt, the Turn of First Shearing, the Turn of the Long Road, and so on. Each turn holds two Closings: one at dusk, one at dawn.

## In stories

In the oldest drovers' songs Ilu is a grandmother who carries water up from under the world in a jar so heavy she can only walk; [[Thane]] is the grandchild who runs ahead and keeps coming back to hurry her. When they arrive together, she pours, and that is the Closing.
