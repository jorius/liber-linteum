# The Linen-Keepers

The **Linen-Keepers** record every water debt on Kessari. They were charged with it in YW 15, the year after the Salt Compact, and they have done it ever since, on linen, because paper cracks in the dry.[^paper] There are about forty of them at any one time, living and working in [[The Loom House]] beside the well. They own nothing, vote on nothing, and draw their water from the Courts like everyone else. Their one power is the oldest rule of the Courts: *before any sealing, the Keeper reads the sums*.

## Written twice

Every row a Keeper writes, she writes twice:

1. **In ink**, along the weave, so that anyone can read it.
2. **In knots**, on the cord sewn into the selvedge, so that a Keeper can read it with her eyes shut.

==Ink can be scraped off and written over. A knot can only be cut, and a cut shows.== That is the whole idea.

## Reading a cord

A number is tied as a run of **clusters**, one cluster for each place of the number, highest place first. A cluster of *n* knots means the digit *n*; an empty place is a **gap a thumb wide**. Apprentices learn to read cords blindfolded.

| Number | Cord, highest place first | Read as |
|---:|---|---|
| 60,800 | `●●●●●● · ␣ · ●●●●●●●● · ␣ · ␣` | six, gap, eight, gap, gap |
| 41,200 | `●●●● · ● · ●● · ␣ · ␣` | four, one, two, gap, gap |
| 413 | `●●●● · ● · ●●●` | four, one, three |

Two rules every apprentice learns in her first week:

- **A slipped knot loses a count; it never gains one.** If ink and cord disagree and the cord is *higher*, the ink is wrong.
- **The long knot.** A single long knot at the end of a row means the debt is still open.

For Ama's own reckoning of the ledgers of 396 to 400, with a little script that does the reading for her, see [[Knot Reckoning]].

## Twins

Every ledger is made twice. One copy stays in the Loom House; the **twin** goes down the Salt Road to [[The Glass Annex]] in Glassreach, so that no single fire, flood or thief can take the whole of the Courts' memory.[^twins]

## Ranks and cords

| Rank | Cord at the cuff |
|---|---|
| Apprentice | Plain, undyed, for practice; messages are tied in it |
| Keeper | Her own colour, chosen for her by the house |
| Second | Her colour, and a white thread |
| Eldest | Her colour, and a black thread |

## Strike through, write beneath

Keepers never scrape and never cut. When a line is found to be false, they strike it through so that it can still be read, write the true line beneath, and tie the reason into the cord. *A record of a lie is also a record.* Since 413, six years of Rise rows in the Loom House carry the same note in the margin: **Rewritten in the Dry Year. Restored from the cord.**

> [!quote] Mother Hesk
> I rewrote the ink. I left the knots. I told myself that was courage. It was only a smaller cowardice.

[^paper]: Paper cracks within a season; parchment curls and gets eaten; the oldest linen rolls in the Annex were woven in YW 15, the Keepers' first year, and they still fold.
[^twins]: The idea is borrowed, the Keepers admit, from the glass-cutters of Tamsk, who always cut two of everything in case one breaks.
