# The Story

> [!quote] The first thing every Linen-Keeper is taught
> Paper cracks in the dry. Parchment curls, and the wind carries off ash. Only linen remembers.

On the desert world of [[Kessari]], water is counted in drams and every dram is owed to someone. The one deep well, the [[Well of Osk]], belongs to the seven merchant houses of [[The Salt Courts]], and every debt drawn from it is written twice by [[The Linen-Keepers]]: once in ink, and once in knots.

[[Ama Seyru]] is nineteen, an apprentice Keeper, and the daughter of a Keeper who was lost on the Salt Road twelve years ago. One night, mending an old ledger by lamplight, she finds that its ink and its knots disagree, and that someone has been patiently rewriting the past in her mother's hand. With the Courts about to cut the water of the glass city of Glassreach, she has twenty-seven days, until the next Dusk Closing, to find out who.

> [!info]- The whole plot in five lines (spoilers)
> Ama finds that the Rise rows of two old ledgers were rewritten to show the well slowly failing.
> On the Salt Road, the quartermaster [[Dela Fenn]] gives her six years of cask tallies her mother taught her to keep.
> In Glassreach the untouched twin ledgers prove the forgery, and [[Teodor Varn]] all but admits why her mother died.
> At the Dusk Closing, [[Mother Hesk]] confesses, and the water rises to the old salt line, past a secret bore cut by House Varn.
> At the low-moon sitting Ama reads the knots aloud, the cut is struck, and House Varn's seat goes dark until its debt is paid.
