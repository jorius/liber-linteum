# Naming brief: a pre-Columbian name for "Notes In My Way"

Research date: 2026-10-20. Meanings checked against dictionaries, academic or museum pages, and UNESCO where possible. Collision checks are quick web searches plus registry RDAP lookups. "Taken" means the domain is registered. "Free" means the registry returned 404, so it is probably available, though it could be reserved or sold at a premium price. **Nothing here is a trademark clearance.** Before you commit, run a real search on USPTO, EUIPO, SIC (Colombia) and IMPI (Mexico).

---

## 1. Candidates

Pronunciation: **EN** is a respelling for English speakers, **ES** is for Spanish speakers. Stressed syllables are in CAPS.

### Nahuatl (Mexica / Aztec; still spoken in Mexico)

**Amatl.** Means **paper**, and also the fig tree whose bark was made into it. Karttunen also gives "letter, book, document." This is the closest equivalent to "Papirus" in the Americas.
- EN: AH-maht'l. The final *tl* is a single sound, so most English speakers will say "AM-at-ul". ES: Á-matl. The Spanish loanword *amate* is familiar to Mexicans and tourists.
- Pitfalls: people will write "amate" or "amalt".
- Sensitivity: low. Amate paper is a living craft, mainly of Otomí papermakers in San Pablito, Puebla. Give them a nod in the About page.
- Sources: [Wired Humanities Nahuatl dictionary](https://nahuatl.wired-humanities.org/content/amatl), [MFA CAMEO](https://cameo.mfa.org/wiki/Amatl).

**Amoxtli.** Means **book, codex**. Molina (1571) glosses it as "libro de escriptura". This is the most on-the-nose meaning for a notes app.
- EN: ah-MOSH-tlee, because *x* = "sh". ES: a-MÓSH-tli. Most Spanish speakers will read the *x* as "ks", as in *México*.
- Pitfalls: the variants *amostli* and *amuxtli* exist, and English speakers will say "a-MOX".
- Sensitivity: low.
- Sources: [Wired Humanities](https://nahuatl.wired-humanities.org/content/amoxtli), [Wiktionary](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/amoxtli).

**Amoxcalli.** Means "**house of books**", a library or archive of codices. Molina glosses it as "bookstore or paper store". It is a good fit for a vault.
- EN: ah-mosh-KAH-lee. ES: a-mosh-CA-li.
- Pitfalls: long, with *x* and double *l*.
- Sources: [Wired Humanities](https://nahuatl.wired-humanities.org/node/173789), [UCM](https://www.ucm.es/quidestliber/amoxoaque).

**Tlacuilo.** Means **scribe-painter**. Molina has "escribano o pintor", and Karttunen has "one who writes or paints".
- EN: tlah-KWEE-loh. The opening *tl-* is hard for English speakers. ES: tla-CUI-lo.
- Sources: [Wired Humanities](https://nahuatl.wired-humanities.org/node/172706).

**Tlamatini.** Means **sage**, literally "one who knows things", from *mati* "to know".
- EN: tlah-mah-TEE-nee. ES: tla-ma-TI-ni.
- Sources: [Wikipedia (Karttunen, Lockhart, Molina)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tlamatini).

**In tlilli in tlapalli.** Means "**the black (ink), the red / the colors**". It is the classic metaphor for writing, the codices and the wisdom of the *tlamatinime*. Scholars dispute the "red" reading: *tlapalli* may simply mean "color".
- EN: TLEEL-lee tlah-PAHL-lee. ES: TLI-li tla-PA-li.
- Too long for the app's name. It is a lovely name for a feature or for the theme system, for example a black-and-red default theme.
- Sources: [SciELO (Estudios de Cultura Náhuatl)](https://www.scielo.org.mx/pdf/ecn/v42/v42a15.pdf), [Kerpel, Matheson Trust](https://www.themathesontrust.org/papers/primordial/Kerpel-Tlamatinime_Ink.pdf).

### Maya (still spoken in Mexico, Guatemala and Belize)

**Huun / Hu'un.** Means **bark paper, and by extension book**. Houston reads it as "paper", then "book", "headband" and "diadem".
- EN: "hoon". ES: "jun", with a soft *j*. Spanish speakers may treat the *h* as silent and say "uun".
- Pitfalls: the glottal-stop apostrophe in *hu'un* should be dropped in a brand name.
- Sensitivity: low.
- Sources: [Maya Decipherment (Houston)](https://mayadecipherment.com/2012/10/06/diadems-in-the-rough/), [Hamburg CSMC](https://www.csmc.uni-hamburg.de/publications/aom/023-en.html).

**Tz'ib (tz'ihb).** Means **writing / painting**. Scribes carried the title *aj tz'ihb*.
- EN: "tseeb". The *tz'* is ejective. ES: "tsib".
- Pitfalls: the apostrophe. "Tzib" without it reads fine.
- Sources: [Stuart, Maya Decipherment](https://mayadecipherment.com/2017/05/01/a-note-on-the-sign-for-tzihb-writing-painting/), [Cambridge, Ancient Mesoamerica](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/46BF5039EEE203448CAFCA7580D07689).

**Chilam (Balam).** Means roughly "**jaguar priest / prophet**". It names a group of colonial Yucatec books of history and prophecy.
- Not recommended. The meaning is about prophecy rather than notes, and the name belongs to living Yucatec religious texts.
- Sources: [UNC exhibit](https://exhibits.lib.unc.edu/exhibits/show/maya/sacred-texts/chilam-balam-of-chumayel), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilam_Balam).

### Quechua / Inca

**Khipu / Quipu.** Means **knotted-cord records**. Khipus held both numbers and narrative, and readers called *khipukamayuq* interpreted them. Cords linked to cords make the best metaphor on this list for linked pages and the Global View graph.
- EN: KEE-poo. ES: "QUI-pu". *Quipu* is the usual Spanish spelling.
- Pitfalls: two spellings (khipu/quipu), plus a sound-alike in **Quip** (Salesforce's docs app).
- Sensitivity: low to medium. Some Peruvian communities still keep khipus as ancestral patrimony.
- Sources: [British Museum](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/E_Am-9941), [Museo Chileno de Arte Precolombino](https://precolombino.cl/wp/en/exposiciones/exposiciones-temporales/exposicion-quipu-contar-anudando-en-el-imperio-inka-2003/), [Smithsonian](https://insider.si.edu/?p=37056).

**Qillqa.** Means **writing, graphic sign**. Wikipedia cites Laime Ajacopa (2007) and the Academia Mayor de la Lengua Quechua (2005).
- EN: KEELL-kah. The uvular *q* means English speakers will say "KILL-ka", which does not sound good for a notes app. ES: "QUÍL-ca".
- Pitfalls: the Spanish spellings *quilca* and *quillca* exist.
- Sensitivity: low.
- Sources: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qillqa). The gloss is second-hand; I did not open the dictionaries themselves.

**Yachay.** Means **knowing, knowledge**. Early colonial lexicons use it for practical, crafted knowledge. The Inca school was the *Yachaywasi*, the "house of knowledge".
- EN: YAH-chai. ES: YA-chai.
- Pitfalls: none. It is easy to say.
- Sources: [Cambridge, *Knowledge and Learning in the Andes*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/knowledge-and-learning-in-the-andes/transmission-of-knowledge-through-textiles-weaving-and-learning-how-to-live/359A2FFA6B5D7730263D14BBBD09E706).

**Amauta.** Means **sage / teacher**. Amautas taught the nobility at the Yachaywasi.
- EN: ah-MOW-tah. ES: a-MAU-ta.
- Sensitivity: low, but the name is heavily used in Peru: Mariátegui's journal *Amauta*, schools and companies.
- Sources: [Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/humanities/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/amauta).

### Colombia

**Pque (Muisca, Muysccubun).** Means **memory**, tied to *pquyquy*, "heart". This comes from a study of the missionary dictionaries of 1600–1619.
- **Unverified.** I could not open the source (HTTP 403), and the gloss reached me only through a search summary.
- It is also unpronounceable as a brand. The Muisca language is extinct, though people are working to revive it.
- Sources: [Cervantes Virtual](https://cervantesvirtual.com/portales/yvan_lissorgues/obra/la-memoria-en-los-muiscas-del-siglo-xvii-una-aproximacion-linguistica-1160695/). I found no better verified Muisca word for thought, paper or writing, so **this slot is effectively dropped.**

**Aluna (Kogi, Sierra Nevada de Santa Marta).** Means **thought, mind, the spiritual world** that comes before matter and sustains it. A film press kit glosses it as "idea, consciousness, thought and essence".
- EN/ES: a-LOO-na. It is easy to say, and Spanish speakers will hear *luna*, "moon".
- **High appropriation risk.** Aluna is a central, living sacred concept. The ancestral knowledge system of the Arhuaco, Kankuamo, Kogi and Wiwa was added to UNESCO's Representative List in 2022, and the Kogi are famously protective of their knowledge. Do not use it without explicit consent from the peoples' own organizations.
- Sources: [Wikipedia: Kogi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kogi_people), [*Aluna* press kit](https://www.thefilmcollaborative.org/films/img/epk/Press_Kit_Aluna_040813.pdf), [UNESCO 17.COM 7.b.3](https://ich.unesco.org/en/Decisions/17.COM/7.b.3).

**Pütchipü'ü (Wayuu, La Guajira).** Means **palabrero**, the "messenger of the word" who mediates disputes.
- Not usable. It is a living judicial office that UNESCO lists as intangible heritage (2010), and the spelling is unbrandable.
- Sources: [UNESCO](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/wayuu-normative-system-applied-by-the-ptchipchi-palabrero-00435).

### Guaraní (Paraguay, where it is an official language)

**Kuatia.** Means **paper, document**. The Paraguayan Guaraní word for book, *kuatiañe'ẽ*, is built on it. Some Bolivian textbooks use it as a verb meaning "to write".
- EN: kwah-tee-AH. Guaraní stresses the last syllable, but English speakers will say kwa-TEE-a. ES: kua-tiá.
- Pitfalls: people may type "quatia" or "kuattia".
- Sensitivity: low, since it is an everyday word.
- Sources: [Wiktionary](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/kuatia), [UBA journal on *kuatiañe'ẽ*](https://publicaciones.sociales.uba.ar/index.php/revistaparaguay/article/download/10041/8649).

**Arandu.** Means **wisdom, intellect, cultural heritage**. As an adjective it means "wise, learned". Krivoshein & Acosta, *Ñe'ẽryru* (2016), p. 17.
- EN: ah-rahn-DOO. ES: a-ran-DÚ. The Spanish spelling is *arandú*.
- Pitfalls: in Colombia it reads almost like **Aranda Software** (see section 2).
- Sources: [Wiktionary (cites dictionaries)](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/arandu), [Greenpeace Brasil](https://www.greenpeace.org/brasil/florestas/arandu-temos-muito-o-que-aprender-com-a-sabedoria-indigena/).

### Mapuche (Mapudungun; Chile and Argentina)

**Kimün.** Means **knowledge, wisdom**: "to know, learn, feel". A wise person is a *kimche*.
- EN: kee-MUHN, where *ü* is a high central vowel. ES: ki-MÜN.
- Pitfalls: the umlaut. The plain-ASCII "Kimun" loses it.
- Sensitivity: medium. Mapuche knowledge and land are a live political issue in Chile.
- Sources: [Silene ONG / Ñanculef, *Tayiñ Mapuche Kimün*](https://www.silene.ong/en/documentation-centre/espistemologia-mapuche-sabiduria-y-conocimientos), [Revista Prospectiva (Univalle)](https://revistaprospectiva.univalle.edu.co/index.php/prospectiva/article/download/14846/18977/75967).

### Mixtec (Ñuu Dzaui) and Zapotec

**Tutu (Mixtec).** Means **paper / book**, as in *tutu nuu dzuma*, "Mexican paper".
- Sources: Jansen's analysis of Alvarado (1593), quoted on [Oudijk's Academia page](https://unam1.academia.edu/micheloudijk). This is a secondary source.
- Unbrandable: the ballet tutu and Desmond Tutu swamp every search.

**Zapotec: dropped.** I could not verify a Zapotec word for paper or book.

### Taíno (Caribbean)

**Areíto.** A ceremonial song and dance that **recounted the deeds of ancestors and chiefs**: memory kept through performance. The fit with a notes app is weak, since the Taíno had no writing.
- EN: ah-ray-EE-toh. ES: a-re-Í-to.
- Sensitivity: medium, because it is ceremonial. The word also has several other uses (see the disambiguation page).
- Sources: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Are%C3%ADto). This is a secondary source only.

## 4. Top-5 shortlist

1. **Amatl**: "paper" in Nahuatl, so it is literally the Mesoamerican Papirus. It is short and distinctive, *amate* already rings a bell in Spanish, the cultural risk is low and amatl.app is free. The only real cost is that English speakers will mangle the *tl*.
2. **Huun**: Maya bark paper, which became the word for "book". This is the "paper becomes book" story in four letters. It is calm and easy to say ("hoon"). huun.dev is free, while .com and .app are registered but empty, so they may be buyable.
3. **Kuatia**: Guaraní for paper or document. It is easy in both languages and has no software clash. A less-obvious, non-Mexican choice.
4. **Amoxtli**: Nahuatl for book or codex, the strongest meaning for a notes app. Its .com, .app and .dev are all registered, and amoxtli.app shows a placeholder, so someone may be planning a product. Check before you commit.
5. **Qillqa**: Quechua for writing. It gives Andean balance, and qillqa.app and .dev are free. The spelling is hard, and it reads "kill-ka" in English.

**Use as feature names instead:**
- **Khipu** for the Global View graph. The cords-of-knots image fits linked pages very well, but Khipu SpA rules it out as the product name.
- ***Tlilli Tlapalli*** for the theme engine or a black-and-red theme.
- ***Amoxcalli*** for the vault or library.

## 5. About "Papirus"

- **Meaning:** papyrus, from Greek *pápyros* through Latin *papyrus*. In Spanish it is *papiro*. It is Egyptian and Mediterranean, not pre-Columbian.
- **Collisions:**
  - [Papirus icon theme](https://github.com/PapirusDevelopmentTeam/papirus-icon-theme), one of the most widely installed Linux icon themes and packaged in Arch, Fedora, Debian, Ubuntu and others. A desktop notes app sharing the name will be confused with it on Linux.
  - **papirus.app** is already a French HR product, a digital logbook for talent development.
  - **papirus.com** belongs to a Brazilian paperboard maker.
  - It sounds like the [Papyrus typeface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papyrus_(typeface)), which is widely mocked (the *Avatar* logo and the SNL sketch).
- **Verdict:** a good idea, a crowded name. **Amatl** or **Huun** carry the same "paper" idea and come from the Americas.

**Caveats:**
- The Quechua *qillqa*, the Mixtec *tutu*, the Muisca *pque* and the Taíno *areíto* glosses come from secondary sources.
- The domain status comes from RDAP lookups on 2026-10-20. "Free" can still be a premium or reserved domain.
- There is no trademark clearance here, so search USPTO, EUIPO, SIC and IMPI before choosing.
- For any word from a living community, consider asking a speaker or community organization. This matters most for the Colombian options.
